### II. LITERATURE SURVEY

To contextualize our architecture, we conducted an extensive survey of fifteen foundational and contemporary research works from prominent NLP venues (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, ICLR, Nature, AAAI, and npj Digital Medicine). We categorize these works across four methodological paradigms: (A) Factuality Benchmarks and Evaluation Metrics, (B) Reference-Free and Representation-Based Detection, (C) Retrieval-Augmented and Verification Systems, and (D) Decoding-Time Mitigations and Systematic Surveys.

#### A. Factuality Benchmarks and Evaluation Metrics
1. **Min et al. (FActScore, EMNLP 2023) [1]:** Min et al. introduced FActScore, an evaluation metric that breaks long-form biography generations into atomic propositions and verifies each assertion against Wikipedia evidence using prompting or fine-tuned retrieval. Evaluating commercial and open-source models (including InstructGPT and LLaMA), they observed factual precision ranging between 56.4% and 70.0% in open-ended biographical generation. While FActScore established the importance of atomic granularity, it functions strictly as a post-hoc evaluation metric rather than a real-time, low-latency hallucination detector, and remains dependent on full Wikipedia retrieval access.
2. **Maynez et al. (Faithfulness and Factuality in Summarization, ACL 2020) [2]:** Maynez et al. executed a landmark large-scale human study auditing abstractive summarization across RNN, CNN, and early Transformer architectures on the XSum benchmark (500 human-annotated BBC articles). Their findings revealed that over 70% of neural summaries suffered from factual hallucinations, with the highest-performing model producing factual and faithful text only 34.7% of the time. While demonstrating that natural language inference (NLI) metrics correlate substantially better with human factuality judgments than n-gram overlap metrics (ROUGE), their study highlighted that entailment metrics suffer from error propagation when reference texts are incomplete.
3. **Thorne et al. (FEVER Benchmark, NAACL 2018) [3]:** Thorne et al. formalized the canonical fact extraction and verification task through the FEVER dataset, comprising 185,445 human-crafted claims classified as *Supported*, *Refuted*, or *NotEnoughInfo* against Wikipedia page extracts. FEVER established standard three-class classification baselines, scoring pipeline accuracies near 64.21%. However, FEVER claims are short, artificially modified sentences written by human crowdworkers to test fact-checkers, differing fundamentally from the complex, fluent, multi-clause hallucinations generated organically by modern autoregressive LLMs.
4. **Li et al. (HaluEval Benchmark, EMNLP 2023) [4]:** Li et al. constructed HaluEval, a large-scale diagnostic benchmark containing 35,000 paired factual and hallucinated texts across question answering, dialogue, and text summarization generated via ChatGPT and validated by human evaluators. They discovered that off-the-shelf LLMs struggle to detect subtle hallucinations, achieving recognition accuracies that varied widely by task (58.5% on dialogue to 74.3% on general QA). While HaluEval provides a rigorous testbed for multi-document summarization, it exists purely as a benchmark dataset and does not propose an autonomous claim decomposition or retrieval architecture.
5. **Asgari et al. (CREOLA Clinical Safety Framework, npj Digital Medicine 2025) [5]:** Asgari et al. formulated CREOLA, a clinical safety assessment framework incorporating a multi-tier hallucination and omission taxonomy, an ISO-inspired clinical risk matrix, and a dedicated clinician annotation platform evaluated on medical consultation transcripts (PriMock57 and ACI-BENCH). They recorded baseline hallucination rates of 1.5% in medical dialogue summarization, of which 44% were severe clinical inaccuracies, showing that structured prompt engineering substantially mitigated major errors. However, CREOLA relies heavily on expensive manual clinician annotation and was evaluated exclusively on a closed proprietary model (GPT-4).

#### B. Reference-Free and Representation-Based Detection
6. **Manakul et al. (SelfCheckGPT, EMNLP 2023) [6]:** Manakul et al. proposed SelfCheckGPT, a zero-resource black-box hallucination detection framework that evaluates consistency across multiple stochastic generations sampled at temperature $T>0$. By computing sentence-level BERTScore, question-answering overlap, and NLI disagreement across sampled completions, SelfCheckGPT outperformed grey-box baselines on WikiBio passages (achieving an AUC-PR of 82.7%). However, SelfCheckGPT operates without external evidence; consequently, when an LLM possesses entrenched parametric biases and confidently repeats the exact same erroneous token across all stochastic draws, SelfCheckGPT fails completely and cannot cite authoritative evidence.
7. **Farquhar et al. (Semantic Entropy, Nature 2024) [7]:** Farquhar et al. developed Semantic Entropy, an unsupervised method that detects LLM confabulations by clustering sampled responses based on bidirectional entailment and computing entropy over the resulting semantic clusters rather than naive token-level distributions. Evaluated across diverse question answering benchmarks (TriviaQA, CoQA), semantic entropy achieved high AUROC improvements without task-specific training data. Nevertheless, semantic entropy targets ungrounded model uncertainty rather than factual correctness; if an LLM is confidently wrong about an established fact, its semantic entropy remains low, yielding a false negative.
8. **Orgad et al. (Hidden-State Probing, ICLR 2025) [8]:** Orgad et al. investigated the internal representation of factual errors by training linear and non-linear probes on transformer hidden states across intermediate decoder layers. Their findings proved that LLMs frequently encode truthfulness signals in early and middle layer activations, outperforming surface token probabilities in identifying erroneous generations. However, internal probes exhibit poor out-of-distribution generalization across divergent linguistic domains, and their study confirmed that an LLM can internally "know" the truth while still generating a hallucinated surface token due to greedy decoding dynamics.

#### C. Retrieval-Augmented and Verification Systems
9. **Lee and Yu / Gekhman et al. (REFIND, SemEval-2025 Task 3) [9]:** Gekhman et al. and Lee and Yu developed REFIND for SemEval-2025 Task 3, addressing multilingual hallucination detection across nine languages using a Context Sensitivity Ratio (CSR) that measures the divergence of output token probabilities when conditioned on retrieved evidence versus empty contexts. REFIND demonstrated substantial Intersection-over-Union (IoU) gains over span-level baselines on benchmark corpora. However, REFIND focuses strictly on character-level token span localization rather than structured, proposition-level factual verdicts with explicit audit traces.
10. **Mishra et al. (FAVA Detect-and-Edit Model, arXiv 2024) [10]:** Mishra et al. presented FAVA, which established a fine-grained six-type hallucination taxonomy (entity, relation, contradictory, uninvented, invent, and subjective) and trained a retrieval-augmented language model to jointly detect and edit errors on the FavaBench dataset (~1,000 human-annotated samples). FAVA surpassed ChatGPT and GPT-4 in fine-grained error localization and boosted post-editing FActScore by 5% to 10%. However, FAVA depends heavily on synthetic training perturbations that can diverge from naturalistic LLM errors and incurs significant latency due to multi-pass generative editing.
11. **Zheng et al. (MRR-FV Multi-Hop Graph Reasoner, AAAI 2025) [11]:** Zheng et al. introduced MRR-FV, an advanced fact verification framework that couples a generative multi-hop passage retriever with a hierarchical intra- and inter-sentence graph neural network reasoner. Evaluated on the FEVER test set and the complex HoVer multi-hop benchmark, MRR-FV achieved approximately 80.8% label accuracy on FEVER and a macro-F1 of 71.7% on 2-hop claims. While state-of-the-art on multi-step reasoning, MRR-FV suffers from extreme computational overhead and is highly sensitive to retrieval-hop hyperparameters, making it unsuitable for rapid, interactive verification.

#### D. Decoding Mitigations and Systematic Surveys
12. **Chuang et al. (DoLa Contrastive Decoding, ICLR 2024) [12]:** Chuang et al. proposed DoLa (Decoding by Contrasting Layers), an inference-time mitigation technique that contrasts the output logits of higher transformer layers against earlier intermediate layers to downweight superficial linguistic associations and amplify factual knowledge. Testing across TruthfulQA and FactorialQA, DoLa improved LLaMA-65B accuracy by 12 to 17 absolute points without external retrieval or retraining. While effective at generation time, DoLa cannot audit already generated text, cannot cite evidence, and does not evaluate claim-level truthfulness.
13. **Wang et al. (Factuality of Large Language Models: A Survey, EMNLP Findings 2024) [13]:** Wang et al. provided a comprehensive systematic survey of factuality evaluation methodologies, benchmark datasets, and knowledge enhancement mechanisms across open-domain generation, summarization, and dialogue. They mapped the structural trade-offs between intrinsic consistency and external retrieval, providing an overarching taxonomy of LLM hallucinations. As a survey, their work provides foundational orientation but introduces no novel empirical architecture or algorithmic ablation.
14. **Li et al. (LLMs-as-Judges Survey, arXiv 2024) [14]:** Li et al. cataloged methodologies for using advanced LLMs as autonomous evaluators across natural language generation tasks, providing an exhaustive audit of systematic judge biases, including position bias, verbosity bias, and self-enhancement preference. Their survey demonstrated that black-box LLM evaluators exhibit significant inconsistency across subtle logical refutations, directly motivating our decision to deploy a deterministic cross-encoder NLI verifier rather than an ungrounded prompted LLM judge.
15. **Sharma (Retrieval-Augmented Generation Survey, ACM TOIS 2025) [15]:** Sharma presented a comprehensive taxonomy of RAG architectures, classifying designs into retriever-centric, generator-centric, hybrid, and robustness-oriented configurations across multi-hop reasoning benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA). The survey documented precision-versus-latency trade-offs and analyzed self-correcting RAG frameworks (e.g., Self-CRAG). As a meta-analysis, it highlights the open challenge of evidence quality and validates our focus on decoupling retrieval similarity from logical verification.

#### E. Research Evolution and Identified Gap
The chronological evolution of fact verification traces a distinct trajectory: from single-sentence Wikipedia verification (FEVER, 2018) and summarization faithfulness audits (Maynez et al., 2020), to reference-free sampling consistency checks (SelfCheckGPT, Semantic Entropy, 2023–2024) and complex multi-hop graph reasoning (MRR-FV, 2025). 

Across this evolution, a critical methodological gap remains unaddressed: the **Evidence Sufficiency Gap**. In existing retrieval-augmented pipelines, semantic similarity and natural language inference are routinely blended without isolating what each signal contributes. Practitioners frequently assume that high cosine similarity between an extracted claim and an evidence passage is indicative of factual support. However, topical similarity measures lexical closeness, not logical entailment. To our knowledge, no existing work has conducted a controlled, ablated study isolating similarity-only, NLI-only, and dynamically gated verification within a single claim-level architecture.
